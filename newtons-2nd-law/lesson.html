Section 1 of 6 — Core Concepts

Newton's Second
Law of Motion

Why does a shopping cart speed up faster when you push harder? Why is a loaded cart harder to get moving than an empty one? The answer to both questions is the single most important equation in mechanics. 🛒💨

Newton's First Law says a net force is needed to change motion. The Second Law tells you exactly how much motion you get — it turns "force" into a number you can calculate.

💡 The Big Idea

A net force causes acceleration. Bigger net force → bigger acceleration. Bigger mass → smaller acceleration.

The Law in One Equation

When a net external force \(\sum \vec{F}\) acts on an object of mass \(m\), it produces an acceleration \(\vec{a}\):

\[ \vec{a} = \frac{\sum \vec{F}}{m} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \sum \vec{F} = m\,\vec{a} \]
SI Unit of Force
\(1\ \text{N} = 1\ \dfrac{kg \cdot m}{s^2}\)
Direction
\(\vec a\) points the same direction as the net force \(\sum\vec F\)

📐 Three Things the Equation Tells You

\(a \propto F\)
Double the net force → double the acceleration (mass fixed).
\(a \propto \tfrac{1}{m}\)
Double the mass → half the acceleration (force fixed).
↗ same way
The object accelerates in the direction of the net force.

🎛️ Interactive: Push the Block

Change the net force and the mass and watch the block accelerate. Notice how \(a = \dfrac{\sum F}{m}\) controls how quickly it speeds up.

Net Force \(\sum F\): 60 N
Mass \(m\): 10 kg
\(a = \dfrac{\sum F}{m} = \) 6.0 m/s²

It's the Net Force That Matters

The \(\sum\) symbol means "add up all the forces" (as vectors). Two people pulling a 100 kg crate in opposite directions with 100 N and 85 N give a net force of only \(100 - 85 = 15\ N\), so \(a = 15/100 = 0.15\ m/s^2\).

🧭 Always draw a Free-Body Diagram (FBD):

Sketch the object as a dot, draw every force as an arrow, then add them up. Forces that cancel (like weight vs. the floor's support) drop out of the net force.

Apparent Weight & Elevators

Stand on a scale in an elevator. The scale reads the normal force \(N\), not your true weight. Applying \(\sum F = ma\) in the vertical direction (\(N - mg = ma\)) gives:

\[ N = m\,(g + a) \]

Accelerating up (\(a>0\)) → you feel heavier. Accelerating down (\(a<0\)) → you feel lighter. In free fall (\(a=-g\)) → \(N=0\), weightless!

Elevator accel \(a\): 0.0 m/s²

60 kg rider — scale reads 588 N

🌀 What is a "g"?

A "g" is a unit of acceleration equal to gravity's pull: \(1\ g = 9.8\ m/s^2\). Fighter pilots pull "6 g's" \(= 6 \times 9.8 = 58.8\ m/s^2\). To convert: m/s² → g's divide by 9.8; g's → m/s² multiply by 9.8.

🍳 How to Solve Any 2nd-Law Problem

1
Draw a free-body diagram — every force as an arrow.
2
Pick a direction as positive and add the forces to get \(\sum F\).
3
Need acceleration first? Use kinematics (e.g. \(a=\dfrac{\Delta v}{t}\) or \(v^2=v_0^2+2a\Delta x\)).
4
Apply \(\sum F = ma\) — solve for the unknown (\(F\), \(m\), or \(a\)).
5
Check units & direction. A negative answer just means "opposite to positive."

Section 3 of 6 — Worked Example

Example: Three Forces on a Block 🧱

Problem Statement

Three forces act on a 10 kg block: \(F_1 = 60\ N\) pulling left, \(F_2 = 30\ N\) pulling up, and \(F_3 = 20\ N\) pulling right.

(a) What is the net force on the block?
(b) What is its acceleration?
(c) If it starts from rest, what is its velocity after 2.0 s?

Work through each step by clicking the buttons below 👇

1
Add the horizontal forces

Take right as positive

\[ \sum F_x = F_3 - F_1 = 20 - 60 = -40\ N \;(\text{i.e. }40\ N\text{ left}) \]
2
Find the total net force

Combine horizontal & vertical with Pythagoras

\[ \sum F_y = F_2 = 30\ N\ (\text{up}) \] \[ \sum F = \sqrt{(40)^2 + (30)^2} = \sqrt{1600+900} = \boxed{50\ N} \]

💡 A classic 3-4-5 triangle! The net force points up-and-to-the-left.

3
Part (b): acceleration

Apply \(a = \sum F / m\)

\[ a = \frac{\sum F}{m} = \frac{50\ N}{10\ kg} = \boxed{5.0\ m/s^2} \]
4
Part (c): velocity after 2.0 s

Kinematics, starting from rest

\[ v = v_0 + a t = 0 + (5.0)(2.0) = \boxed{10\ m/s} \]
✅ Recap: Net force 50 N → a = 5.0 m/s² → after 2 s the block moves at 10 m/s, in the direction of the net force.

📋 Key Formulas

\(\sum F = ma\)  |  \(a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{t}\)  |  \(v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x\)  |  \(\Delta x = v_0 t + \tfrac12 a t^2\)  |  \(N = m(g+a)\)  |  \(1\,g = 9.8\ m/s^2\)

Group A — Force, Mass & Acceleration

Problem 1

Skelly the skater (total mass 25 kg) is propelled by rocket power.
(a) Neglecting resistance, find the acceleration for a thrust of 100 N and of 200 N. What thrust gives a = 10 m/s²?
(b) Now with a constant 50 N of resistance, find the acceleration for thrusts of 50 N, 100 N, and 200 N.

(a) \(a = F/m\): 100 N → \(\boxed{4\ m/s^2}\); 200 N → \(\boxed{8\ m/s^2}\); for \(a=10\): \(F = ma = 25\times10 = \boxed{250\ N}\)
(b) Net \(= F - 50\): 50 N → \(\boxed{0\ m/s^2}\); 100 N → \(50/25=\boxed{2\ m/s^2}\); 200 N → \(150/25=\boxed{6\ m/s^2}\)
Problem 2

Al and Bea pull a 100.0 kg crate across a frictionless floor in opposite directions: \(F_A = 100\ N\) right, \(F_B = 85\ N\) left.
(a) Net force?   (b) Acceleration?

(a) \(\sum F = 100 - 85 = \boxed{15\ N}\) to the right
(b) \(a = \sum F/m = 15/100 = \boxed{0.15\ m/s^2}\) to the right
Problem 3

A net force of 255 N causes a cart to accelerate at 4.20 m/s². What is the mass of the cart?

\(m = \dfrac{\sum F}{a} = \dfrac{255}{4.20} \approx \boxed{60.7\ kg}\)
Problem 4

A 1000 kg car traveling at 30.0 m/s slows to 10.0 m/s at a steady rate over 60.0 m.
(a) Acceleration?   (b) Net force to slow it?   (c) After it reaches 10.0 m/s and cruises steadily, what is the net force?

(a) \(v^2=v_0^2+2a\Delta x\): \(a = \dfrac{10^2-30^2}{2(60)} = \dfrac{-800}{120} \approx \boxed{-6.67\ m/s^2}\)
(b) \(\sum F = ma = 1000(-6.67) \approx \boxed{-6670\ N}\) (opposite the motion)
(c) Constant velocity → \(a=0\) → \(\boxed{\sum F = 0}\)

Group B — Rockets, Elevators & Apparent Weight

Problem 5

A 20 kg rocket is accelerating straight up at +30.0 m/s². It feels its weight \(F_g\) (down) and thrust \(F_{th}\) (up).
(a) Net force?   (b) Its weight \(F_g\)?   (c) The thrust \(F_{th}\)?

(a) \(\sum F = ma = 20\times30 = \boxed{600\ N}\) up
(b) \(F_g = mg = 20\times9.8 = \boxed{196\ N}\) down
(c) \(F_{th} - F_g = ma \Rightarrow F_{th} = m(g+a) = 20(9.8+30) = \boxed{796\ N}\)
Problem 6

A 10 kg box rides in an elevator.
(a) The elevator accelerates up at +2.0 m/s². What is the net force on the box?
(b) A 60 kg woman in that elevator (accelerating up at +3.0 m/s²) — does she feel heavier, lighter, or normal, and what does a scale read?

(a) \(\sum F = ma = 10\times2.0 = \boxed{20\ N}\) up
(b) Accelerating up → heavier. Scale reads \(N = m(g+a) = 60(9.8+3.0) = \boxed{768\ N}\) (vs. 588 N normally)
Problem 7

Block A sits on a frictionless table, connected by a string over a pulley to Block B, which hangs and falls, dragging A. Both blocks have the same mass \(m\).
(a) What is the mass of the system?   (b) What force accelerates the system?   (c) Show the acceleration is \(g/2\).

(a) System mass \(= 2m\) (both blocks move together)
(b) Only the weight of B, \(F = mg\), pulls the system forward
(c) \(a = \dfrac{F}{m_{sys}} = \dfrac{mg}{2m} = \boxed{\dfrac{g}{2} = 4.9\ m/s^2}\)
Problem 8 — Conceptual

Shirley pushes a heavy crate across a frictionless floor. True or False:
(a) She needs a force to get it moving.
(b) She needs a force to keep it moving at constant velocity.
(c) She needs a force to stop it.

(a) True — to accelerate from rest needs a net force.
(b) False — at constant velocity \(a=0\), so \(\sum F = 0\). No force needed (inertia keeps it going).
(c) True — stopping is a change in velocity (negative acceleration), which needs a net force.
