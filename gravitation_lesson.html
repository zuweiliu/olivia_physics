Section 1 of 6 — Core Concepts

Newton's Universal
Law of Gravitation

What do aching feet, a falling apple, and the Moon's orbit have in common? They are all caused by the same force — gravity. 🍎🌙

In 1687, Isaac Newton was the first to write an exact mathematical law for gravity — and it works everywhere in the universe!

🎮 Watch gravity keep the moon in orbit. The gravitational force = centripetal force!

💡 The Big Idea

Everything pulls on everything else. Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a gravitational force.

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

The force between two masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) separated by distance \(r\):

\[ F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \]
Gravitational Constant
\(G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\ \frac{N \cdot m^2}{kg^2}\)
Direction
Always attractive, directed along the line joining the two masses

Weight vs. Gravitational Constant

Near Earth's surface, weight \(W = mg\). But where does \(g = 9.80\ m/s^2\) come from? Set \(F = mg\) and solve:

\[ g = G\frac{M_E}{r^2} \]

Plug in \(M_E = 5.98 \times 10^{24}\ kg\) and \(r_E = 6.37 \times 10^6\ m\):

\[ g = \left(6.674 \times 10^{-11}\right) \times \frac{5.98 \times 10^{24}}{(6.37 \times 10^6)^2} = 9.80\ m/s^2 \checkmark \]
⚠️ Key Insight:

For objects near Earth's surface, use \(W = mg\). For objects far from Earth, always use \(W = G\dfrac{M_E\, m}{r^2}\).

Section 3 of 6 — Worked Example

Example: Hubble Space Telescope 🔭

Problem Statement

The Hubble Space Telescope has a mass of 11,600 kg and orbits Earth at an altitude of 598 km above Earth's surface.

(a) What is its weight when resting on Earth?
(b) What is its weight while in orbit?

Work through each step by clicking the buttons below 👇

1
Part (a): Weight on Earth's surface

Near Earth, use W = mg

\[ W = mg = 11600\ kg \times 9.80\ m/s^2 \] \[ \boxed{W = 113{,}680\ N \approx 1.14 \times 10^5\ N} \]

💡 On Earth, Hubble weighs about as much as a fully-loaded semi-truck!

2
Part (b): Find the orbital radius r

r = Earth's radius + altitude above surface

\[ r = r_E + h = 6.37 \times 10^6\ m + 598 \times 10^3\ m \] \[ r = 6.37 \times 10^6 + 0.598 \times 10^6 = \boxed{6.968 \times 10^6\ m} \]
3
Part (b): Apply Newton's Law of Gravitation

Far from surface → use \(W = G M_E m / r^2\)

\[ W = G\frac{M_E\, m}{r^2} = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \times \frac{(5.98 \times 10^{24})(11600)}{(6.968 \times 10^6)^2} \] \[ = \frac{6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 6.937 \times 10^{28}}{4.855 \times 10^{13}} \] \[ \boxed{W \approx 9.53 \times 10^4\ N} \]
✅ Compare: On Earth: 113,680 N  |  In orbit: 95,300 N

Gravity in orbit is still significant — it's what keeps Hubble orbiting! (~84% of surface weight)

🤔 Thinking Question

Why is astronauts' apparent weight (what they feel) zero in orbit, even though gravity is still pulling them?

Because the astronaut and the spacecraft are both in free fall together around Earth. There's no normal force — they are in continuous free fall, which creates the sensation of weightlessness!

Group A — Newton's Universal Gravitation

Problem 1

What gravitational force does the Earth exert on the Moon, which orbits at a distance of \(3.8 \times 10^8\ m\)? What force does the Moon exert on the Earth? Explain.

\(F = G\dfrac{M_E M_m}{r^2} = 6.674\!\times\!10^{-11} \times \dfrac{5.98\!\times\!10^{24} \times 7.4\!\times\!10^{22}}{(3.8\!\times\!10^8)^2} \approx \boxed{2.05 \times 10^{20}\ N}\)

By Newton's 3rd Law, the Moon exerts the same magnitude force (2.05×10²⁰ N) back on Earth — forces always come in equal-and-opposite pairs.
Problem 2

A 2.0 kg meteor is attracted to the Earth with a force of 15.0 N.
(a) Find its distance from the center of the Earth.
(b) Find its altitude above the Earth's surface, in kilometers.

(a) \(r = \sqrt{G M_E m / F} = \sqrt{6.674\!\times\!10^{-11} \times 5.98\!\times\!10^{24} \times 2.0\,/\,15.0} \approx \boxed{7.28 \times 10^6\ m}\)
(b) altitude \(= r - r_E = 7.28\!\times\!10^6 - 6.37\!\times\!10^6 = 9.1\!\times\!10^5\ m \approx \boxed{910\ km}\)
Problem 3

Two identical space shuttles docked 90.0 meters apart are attracted to each other with a force of \(9.20 \times 10^{-7}\ N\). Find their masses.

Since \(m_1 = m_2 = m\): \(F = Gm^2/r^2\), so \(m = r\sqrt{F/G}\)
\(m = 90.0 \times \sqrt{9.20\!\times\!10^{-7}\,/\,6.674\!\times\!10^{-11}} = 90.0 \times 117.4 \approx \boxed{1.06 \times 10^4\ kg}\)
Problem 5

A 120 g ball is located at the center of a 8000 kg hollow sphere 100.0 m in radius. The ball is also 50.0 m from the center of a solid, 50.0 kg sphere, 25.0 m in radius. What is the net gravitational force the two spheres exert on the ball?

Hollow sphere → Shell Theorem → 0 N on ball inside it.
Solid sphere: \(F = G\dfrac{m_\text{ball}\,m_\text{solid}}{r^2} = 6.674\!\times\!10^{-11}\times\dfrac{0.120\times50.0}{(50.0)^2} \approx \boxed{1.60\times10^{-13}\ N}\)
Net force = \(1.60\times10^{-13}\ N\) toward the solid sphere.
Problem 6

Three lead balls in a line — net force on the middle ball is zero. A 20.0 kg ball is 4.0 m to the left; an unknown mass \(m\) is 2.0 m to the right. Find \(m\).

Set \(F_\text{left}=F_\text{right}\): \(\dfrac{G\cdot20.0\cdot M}{(4.0)^2} = \dfrac{G\cdot m\cdot M}{(2.0)^2}\)
Cancel \(G\) and \(M\): \(\dfrac{20.0}{16} = \dfrac{m}{4.0}\) → \(m = \boxed{5.0\ kg}\)
Problem 7

Three identical spheres of mass \(m\) at vertices of an equilateral triangle (side \(L\)). Write an expression for the net gravitational force on one sphere due to the other two.

Each of the other two exerts \(F=Gm^2/L^2\); angle between them = 60°.
\(F_\text{net}=\sqrt{F^2+F^2+2F^2\cos60°}=F\sqrt{3}\)
\[\boxed{F_\text{net}=\frac{\sqrt{3}\,Gm^2}{L^2}}\] directed toward the centroid.

Group B — Acceleration Due to Gravity

Problem B1

An astronaut on the Moon holds a feather.
(a) Find \(g\) on the Moon.
(b) Drop the feather from 2.0 m — how long to hit the ground?
(c) The feather weighs 0.020 N on Earth. What is its weight on the Moon?

(a) \(g_\text{moon}=G M_m/r_m^2=6.674\!\times\!10^{-11}\times7.4\!\times\!10^{22}/(1.74\!\times\!10^6)^2\approx\boxed{1.63\ m/s^2}\)
(b) \(t=\sqrt{2h/g}=\sqrt{4.0/1.63}\approx\boxed{1.57\ s}\)
(c) \(W_\text{moon}=0.020\times(1.63/9.80)\approx\boxed{3.3\times10^{-3}\ N}\)
Problem B2

A space shuttle (\(9.3\times10^4\ kg\)) orbits at 350 km altitude.
(a) Distance from Earth's center?
(b) Centripetal acceleration?
(c) Weight of an 85 kg astronaut if at rest at that altitude?

(a) \(r=6.37\!\times\!10^6+350\!\times\!10^3=\boxed{6.72\times10^6\ m}\)
(b) \(g=GM_E/r^2=6.674\!\times\!10^{-11}\times5.98\!\times\!10^{24}/(6.72\!\times\!10^6)^2\approx\boxed{8.83\ m/s^2}\)
(c) \(W=85\times8.83\approx\boxed{750\ N}\)
